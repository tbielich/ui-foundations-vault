# Design Principles

## Question

What makes good design?

## Purpose

Design principles state the durable qualities that make interfaces understandable, usable, and trustworthy.

## Core Principles

- Proximity: place related information, controls, and feedback near each other.
- Hierarchy: make the primary task, current context, and next action easier to find than secondary material.
- Contrast: preserve readable relationships between foreground, background, boundaries, and states.
- Affordance: make available actions and interaction possibilities apparent.
- Simplicity: reduce simultaneous decisions by removing unnecessary structure and exposing only what matters now.
- Consistency: reuse behavior, placement, language, and visual treatment so people can transfer expectations.
- Intentionality: every visible element should have a reason to exist.
- Inclusion: design for varied abilities, contexts, devices, languages, and levels of familiarity.

## Guidance

Prefer principles that clarify tradeoffs over rules that prescribe a single form. A design can be visually polished and still fail if its purpose, structure, or interaction model is unclear.
