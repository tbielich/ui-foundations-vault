# Design Intelligence

## Question

How should designers reason?

## Purpose

Design intelligence is the ability to evaluate whether a design is appropriate, understandable, usable, accessible, coherent, and intentional.

## Core Principle

Good design is not merely correct. A design can satisfy individual rules and still fail if it does not fit the goal, user, context, constraints, or system it belongs to.

## Reasoning Order

Evaluate design in this order:

1. Goal: define the outcome the design should create.
2. User: identify who is using it and what they already know.
3. Context: understand where, when, and under what conditions it is used.
4. Constraints: identify what must be preserved or cannot change.
5. Principles: apply the durable design principles that fit the situation.
6. Heuristics: identify visible usability risks.
7. Critique: evaluate focus, hierarchy, density, clarity, trust, cohesion, and visual noise.

## Judgment Dimensions

- Appropriateness: the solution fits the goal, audience, and situation.
- Complexity: complexity is justified by value, risk, or necessity.
- Tradeoffs: gains, losses, and alternatives are explicit.
- Confidence: the experience feels reliable and understandable.
- Cohesion: parts feel like they belong to the same system.
- Scalability: the decision still makes sense when repeated.

## Guidance

Do not jump from observation to recommendation. Reason through purpose, context, constraints, principles, heuristics, and tradeoffs before proposing change.
